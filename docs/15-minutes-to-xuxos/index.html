15 Minutes to Xuxos

May 14, 2026

Another week, another bakery. And this one is just a 15-minute round trip walk from the office.

Qué Sweet is the pastry and ice cream counter inside Mercado Little Spain, José Andrés' 35,000-square-foot Spanish food hall at Hudson Yards, a collaboration with Ferran and Albert Adrià designed to recreate the logic of a Spanish mercado in Manhattan.

The signature item is the xuxo (pronounced roughly SHOO-sho), a pastry from Girona, in Catalonia, dating to the 1920s. The origin story involves an acrobat, a pastry chef's daughter, a well-timed sneeze, and an exchange of recipes in lieu of getting thrown out of the bakery. Whether or not that's true, the Catalan government has formally recognized the xuxo as a Producte de la Terra, a designation for traditional regional products.

Structurally, it's laminated dough filled with crema catalana-style pastry cream, deep fried, and rolled in sugar. What you get is a small golden cylinder, lightly sugared and slightly blistered from frying, that manages to feel improbably light. The exterior gives a faint crackle. Inside: soft, elastic fried dough and cold pastry cream. It's somewhere between a filled doughnut and a fried croissant, executed with excellent judgment.

Beyond the xuxo, the counter offers crema catalana, Basque-style cheesecake, and soft serve built around Spanish flavor traditions. Desserts that feel intentionally regional rather than trend-chasing. And if you're coming back and really want to go next level, there's the xuxo helado — the same pastry split or filled with soft serve, then finished with sauces or toppings.

What an awesome thing to get out of a 15-minute break. I got three xuxos just in case I felt like I wanted to share and/or give myself an xo later (by way of xuxo, if you will).
