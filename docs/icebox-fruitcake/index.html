Icebox Fruitcake

April 6, 2026

One of my earliest memories of baking is how I hated baking. Although that's not exactly right, I guess. I hated the thing that kept getting baked and pushed in front of me: fruitcake. Ugh. The smell. The look. The texture. The taste. I still can't stand fruitcake. I'll grant that I haven't tried it in years, but something about it... some weird, candied fruit is ALWAYS in a fruitcake. I can't put my finger on it. I like everything that people typically put in a fruitcake, but when you put the ingredients together and put in to bake, what comes out is godawful. Oh, a big caveat - I don't like raisins. Maybe that's the thing. Maybe the raisins all melt out in the oven and then gel everything together to make one big sticky raisin log. Wait, is that how a fruitcake works? Is that the appeal? I really don't understand that. I understand Stuckey's peanut “logs” if you’re going for trunk shaped desserts. (If you don't know what they are, go on a cross-country road trip and stop at a Stuckey's. Brilliant.) But a raisin log with weird other "fruit" tastes is not cool.

My grandma was a good-hearted woman. So, she baked the gel raisin log for the lowbrow members of the family, but she also made a concoction called an "icebox fruitcake” for just me and her. In one way maybe she could've been trying take the sting out of the word "fruitcake" itself, to ease me into the word - like maybe I'd come to like the "real" ones if I didn't have a negative reaction to their name. But I don't think that's the case. I think she secretly hated the conventional, terrible version, too, and wanted a reason to make the icebox ones. Maybe she was a shade of iconoclast, too, in an oh so gentle, subversive Southern way. Going against tradition, fighting the powers, making icebox fruitcake. Our secret anti-establishment complicity. Man, I loved my grandma.

If you've never had it or never made it, you must. The "icebox" part means you don't have to bake it. You just mix everything together, put it in a loaf pan (best presentation), and put it in the refrigerator to cool. È squisito.

What do you need?

Graham Crackers — Never saw anything in the house besides Honey Maid. Didn't know there were even other brands. Crush them in a ziplock bag with a rolling pin or iron or whatever heavy you've got. Be careful not to burst the bag.

Chopped Pecans — I grew up in the south, so pecans were easy to find. NYC? They might be super expensive. Use walnuts if you can't find pecans. Some folks swear by almonds. I will swear at them. My grandma would have, too. This is not an almond dish.

Maraschino Cherries — Love these. You get to use a whole jar. Maybe this is why kid me liked this so much. When shopping for this recipe, buy two jars. Snack on one while you prepare the icebox fruitcake so you don't eat the ones that are supposed to go in the dessert.

Mini Marshmallows — Maybe little me liked this even more than the maraschino cherries. These are super essential.

Shredded Sweetened Coconut — You don't like coconut? Okay. Don't know what to tell you. No substitute. Go home.

Sweetened Condensed Milk — I always remember that little Carnation can. Never knew what else it was used for till I was an adult. My grandma didn't know about boiling the can itself to make Dulce de Leche, but I know she would've liked it. If you haven't done it, it's something you have to try. And Tres Leches. And Key Lime Pie. Also, a must in the Vietnamese iced coffee - a new shop just opened a block or so from me.

Raisins — Some recipes call for raisins. Raisins have already been discussed.

What do you do with all that?

Lightly spray two loaf pans with nonstick cooking spray. Crush the graham crackers using a food processor or add them to a large ziptop bag and crush using a rolling pin. Coarsely chop the cherries.

Combine the crushed graham crackers, pecans, cherries, coconut, marshmallows, and sweetened condensed milk in a large bowl and mix well. Add reserved cherry juice by the tablespoonful until the mixture comes together but isn't too soft. Between 2 and 3 tablespoons.

Turn out into the prepared pans and press flat with your hands. Cover and chill at least 6 hours, then cut into bars. Keep refrigerated.

Thank you, grandma, for the icebox fruitcake. I kind of always thought it was baked until I looked it up later in life. All good. Era di altissima qualità. So was she.
