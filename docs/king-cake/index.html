King Cake

February 17, 2026

"There's this little store down the road that sure makes a good king cake. I can just go to Walmart and get a pretty good one actually, but the one down there is pretty premium. If you were down here I'd show you. Tastes good. They've been doing them for a long time. Pretty well known. Famous for king cakes. Not far from where I live. They're pretty big. Better than Walmart I think. Though Walmart is pretty good. I just got one from Walmart this weekend. Saturday maybe. I had to go over there to pick up some things. I was walking through the produce section, and I saw the king cakes and put one in the cart. Their produce section is pretty good. And so is their bakery for some stuff. I guess only some stuff for their produce, though; I like their tomatoes. Their lettuce is horrible. It's hard to get a good tomato at a grocery store. You know that place down the road, can't think of the name, with the king cakes: they have good tomatoes when they're in season. Local produce. Mostly soybeans around here, but some good tomatoes, too. I was going to grow some tomatoes last year; I did some a couple of years ago. Came out with some good green ones we had on wheat bread with mayonnaise and salt and pepper for good tomato sandwiches. Never do the fried green tomatoes, but I've had some good ones. Man, you know? Those king cakes are good."

"Where are you now?" I asked my dad.

"Oh I was at home when you called. I'm in the car now going to get a king cake and see if that store down the road has any good produce. I'll text you the name of the store later if I can remember."

People were staring at the plastic cake carrier on the subway as I was bringing in my king cake to work. The carrier is see-through, so everyone could see the cakes with their gold, purple, and green sugar-sprinkled frosting. I'd like to imagine that they were covetous of the cake, wishing they could have a bite, but they were probably just annoyed. You're taking up too much room, get out of the way. Maybe I should've taken a Lyft.

I figured I could make a king cake. I figured I should make a king cake. Being from Mobile, Ala., the home of the first Mardi Gras celebration in America (not New Orleans, Mobilians are sensitive about that), I thought it'd only be right that I tried once, at least. Maybe it'd be on par with a local Louisiana bakery? The bar was high according to my father. At least better than Wal-Mart. But at the very least it'd be homemade by me and there are points for that, and I could always blame a screw-up on the recipe. I'd bring it in to work, because what did I need with a bunch of king cake at home. Also, I'd be crowned the office authority on king cakes.

Indeed, an authoritative primer for coworkers:

A King cake is a seasonal cake tied to Epiphany (January 6) and Mardi Gras, taking its name from the biblical Magi. In the U.S. Gulf Coast, the tradition dates to Basque settlers in 1718 and is now closely associated with Carnival season — celebrated from Mobile, Ala., home of America's first Mardi Gras, across to East Texas. The classic Louisiana version is a ring of cinnamon roll-style dough decorated in Mardi Gras colors — purple (justice), green (faith), and gold (power). A small plastic baby is hidden inside; whoever finds it gets good luck and the obligation to buy next year's cake.

I didn't have a recipe for one, so I went online and just searched "king cake recipe." Twenty million hits. I started sifting through the first hundred or so (seriously). I began eliminating many of them pretty quickly. Too pretentious. Too complicated. Too fancy. Needs a Kitchen Aid stand mixer (no, I'm a Cuisinart guy).

It came down to three recipes, one from a "Louisiana baker," one from allrecipes.com, and one from Epicurious. Epicurious used the most butter and didn't include any bastardization (Cream cheese? Really?), so I went with that one.

When I finally got around to making the cake, it was late on a Sunday night. I fell asleep before I could finish the frosting and had to wake up at 5am to get everything done before work. So I frosted it, sprinkled it, babied it, boxed it up, and took the subway.

But before I put the king cake out at the office, I had to make a warning sign about the plastic babies in the cakes. King cakes aren't a thing in NYC. It's not like everybody would know that they could choke on something inside of a braided cinnamon cake.

The warning in verse:

Please note
There is a plastic baby
In this Mardi Gras king cake.
It symbolizes good
Luck! Be careful
Not to bite into it.
Let me know if you get
The baby

Laissez les bon temps rouler!

After trying some cake, my coworker asked me, "So, you sent one of these to your father, right?"

"Maybe next year," I said. "You think he would like it?"

She is a pretty serious baker, taking her cakes to competitions around the country. She takes a week off from work every year to go to baking conferences. I hold her opinion in very high regard.

"I like it. You did good," she said. "He will love it. You're his son. He should know what his son can do. His opinion matters more than mine, but I think you got it perfectly."

I texted him a photo of my finished cake to let him know that, even outside of the South, I could indeed have some king cake for Mardi Gras.

"Looks good."

Then:

"Walmart king cakes not too good, though. They have some specialty bakers that do a real good job at Woods. It's the one here in Alexandria. Hope you have a great Mardi Gras."
