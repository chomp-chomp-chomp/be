On the Divergent Names of American Cookies and British Biscuits
The British–American split between “biscuit” and “cookie” is not simply a charming naming quirk but a kind of cultural symptom: each nation projects its own neurotic need for order or indulgence onto a fragile baked object, as if history itself were demanding a verdict on crispness versus softness. And the historical record only reveals that our attempts to stabilize meaning crumble like the pastries themselves, exposing how language is always already betraying us.
