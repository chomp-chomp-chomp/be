Blind Baking

March 31, 2026

I bake when I am sad. It's hard to bake when you're frustrated. So I stay sad instead. Sadness brings cakes.

When I feel like crying I go the refrigerator and pull out a couple of eggs and the butter. Room temperature eggs whip better. You only get 50-60% volume with cold eggs. You want a fluffy cake. Usually. And how can you cream hard butter with the sugar? There are tricks to get the eggs up to temp, but I haven't found a good one to get butter to the consistency I really want. There's the whole microwave burst thing, but some of the butter always melts a bit. It's close. But not the best. So I sit with my sadness at room temperature.

I pull out the correct measuring spoons for the various ingredients. I sort them in step order. 1 tsp. 1 tsp. 1 tbsp. 1/2 tsp. 1 tbsp.

I pull out the mixing bowl. I get the scale. Put a bowl on it. Tare. Add the flour. I rarely use the right measuring cups anymore. One leveled cup of flour never equals 224 mg of flour. Sometimes I just use a big spoon to scoop whatever and let the scale scale.

I use four lemons for three tablespoons of zest. It feels weird just using the lemons for their outer layer and then throwing the rest away. I typically put the naked lemons in ziplock bags and put them in the refrigerator, but then I forget about them and then just throw them away a month later instead. The kitchen smells like something is about to get better.

I cream the butter and sugar and zest together. Three to five minutes on medium in my Cuisinart stand mixer. People rave about Kitchen Aid. Ooh, they last forever! they say. Nope. I'm a Cuisinart person. They've always done me right. The creaming process works - yellow and dense to something pale and soft and almost hopeful looking.

I add the eggs one at a time. I crack each one into a separate little Pyrex first. Then I tip the little bowl into the mixing bowl, the machine running on medium-low. I don't want my fingers to get caught in the mixer, but this is the step. Always follow the steps. I watch the raw egg as it breaks apart and twirls into the buttersugarzest.

The sour cream goes in with the lemon juice. It looks reluctant to mix for a second but then it comes together.

I fold in the flour in three parts. Low speed. You don't want to overwork it. God, I am not gluten-free, therefore, I will allow gluten to bind this stuff together.

It bakes for 55 minutes. That's a long time for a cake, but maybe I guess not. Normal, I guess. I stand in the kitchen. I stand by the stove. I walk to the sink. I stand by the sink. Back and forth. I'm still sad and I don't want to sit down. I wash the dirty dishes. I stand.

The syrup is lemon juice and sugar cooked down until the sugar dissolves. I make it just before taking out the cake. Then I brush it on while the cake is still warm. It soaks in and the top goes a little tacky and dark and perfect. I do the sides too. Then I do it again. The recipe says a third at first and the rest after. I follow the recipe exactly.

The glaze is just powdered sugar and lemon juice whisked until it moves like not concrete and not water. Somewhere in-between. Maybe like lava? Kinda concrete-y, but seems smooth. I pour it over when the cake is cool.

Lemon pound cakes are for sharing. For company, you know? But I don't have anyone to share it with.

I don't even eat any of it. I just let it sit there. Maybe I'll have a piece in a couple of days.

I'm so sad I can't even see.
